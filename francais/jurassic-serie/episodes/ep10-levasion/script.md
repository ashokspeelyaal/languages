## Pause ! · La maison d'être

- **alan (EN, board maison)**: Pause! Listen again to Owen. «Il est sorti de l'enclos.»² «Il est allé dans la forêt.»² In episode nine the past used «avoir». Now look: «il est», not «il a». [[+]]Here is the rule. A small group of verbs, mostly verbs of movement, use «être» in the past, not «avoir». The formula: «être», plus the participle. Picture a house, the house of être. Why these verbs? Because they describe going somewhere, or a change: you enter, you leave, you fall, you are born. Many come in pairs of opposites, which makes them easy to remember. [[+]]At the door: «entrer», «entré». «Sortir», «sorti».² In, and out. [[+]]On the stairs: «monter», «monté». «Descendre», «descendu».² Up, and down. [[+]]The road: «arriver», «arrivé». «Partir», «parti».² Arrive, and leave. [[+]]«Aller», «allé». «Venir», «venu». «Revenir», «revenu». «Retourner», «retourné».² To go, to come, to come back, to go back. [[+]]«Tomber», «tombé». «Rester», «resté».² To fall, to stay. [[+]]And two for life: «devenir», «devenu», to become. «Naître», «né», to be born. The negative wraps around «être»: «Je ne suis pas sorti.»² Everything else, you know: «j'ai mangé», «j'ai vu», with «avoir». Learn the house, and you know the exceptions. Say the pairs aloud: «entrer, sortir; monter, descendre; arriver, partir; aller, venir.»² Remember: the house is small, and everything outside it uses «avoir».
- **À toi**: Say: She left. -> Elle est partie.

## Pause ! · L'accord

- **ellie (EN, board accord)**: Pause! Ashok forgot an «e». Here is the big difference from «avoir». With «être», the participle agrees with the subject, exactly like an adjective. [[+]]You already know adjectives: add «e» for feminine, «s» for plural. The participle is like an adjective that describes the subject. [[+]]«Il est parti.»² But: «Elle est partie.»² With an «e». [[+]]«Ils sont partis.»² «Elles sont parties.»² With «s» and «es». [[+]]«Nous sommes arrivés.»² Here, Alan, Claire and Ashok: a mixed group is masculine plural, so «arrivés». Even one man in a group of ten women makes it masculine: «Ils sont arrivés.»² Only an all-female group is feminine: «Claire et Ellie sont arrivées.»² [[+]]And «je»? It depends on who speaks. Ashok says: «Je suis tombé.»² A woman says: «Je suis tombée.»² In speech you cannot hear the «e», but in writing you must add it. Same idea for «tu»: «Tu es arrivé»² for a man, «tu es arrivée»² for a woman. «Il est parti» and «elle est partie» sound the same: only your writing shows the difference.
- **À toi**: Say: They stayed in the jeep. Ils, masculine. -> Ils sont restés dans la jeep.

## Pause ! · Le, la, les

- **ellie (EN, board pron)**: Pause! Ashok said: «Je le vois.»² «Je la vois.»² «Je les vois.»² These little words replace a noun, so you do not repeat it. «Le, la, l', les» are direct object pronouns. [[+]]And there is one big surprise for English speakers: they go before the verb. [[+]]«Je vois le raptor.»² becomes «Je le vois.»² «Le» replaces a masculine word. It means him, or it. [[+]]«Je vois la jeep.»² becomes «Je la vois.»² «La» is feminine: her, or it. [[+]]«Je vois les traces.»² becomes «Je les vois.»² «Les» is plural: them. [[+]]The negative wraps around the whole block: «Je ne le vois pas.»² Pronoun after «ne», verb, then «pas». [[+]]In the past, the pronoun stays before «avoir»: «Je l'ai vu.»² «Le» becomes «l'» before a vowel. And with an infinitive, it goes before the infinitive: «Il faut le trouver.»² We must find him. And for a question, nothing special: «Tu le vois?»² Do you see him? «Tu la vois?»² «Tu les vois?»² Same order as the statement: pronoun, then verb.
- **À toi**: Say: Do you see the T. rex? Yes, I see it. -> Oui, je le vois.

[[+]]With «le», masculine, nothing changes: «Je l'ai vu.»² [[+]]But with «la», feminine, the participle gets an «e»: «Je l'ai vue.»² You will not hear it. You will see it. [[+]]With «les»: «Je les ai vus.»² And feminine plural: «Je les ai vues.»² [[+]]The rule: with «avoir», the participle agrees with the pronoun only when the pronoun comes BEFORE the verb.
