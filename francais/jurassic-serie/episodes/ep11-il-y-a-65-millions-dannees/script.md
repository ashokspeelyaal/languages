## Pause ! · L'imparfait  (ellie)
- **ellie (EN)**: Pause! Look at the verbs Alan used: «vivaient, faisait, avait, mangeaient, creusais.» All end in «ai» or «ait» or «aient». This is the imparfait: the tense for describing the past. And it is the easiest tense in French to build. Step one: take the «nous» form of the present. «Nous regardons.» Remove the «ons»: you keep «regard». Step two: add the endings: «ais, ais, ait, ions, iez, aient.»² Say it with me: «ais, ais, ait, ions, iez, aient.»² So: «je regardais, tu regardais, il regardait»² and «nous regardions, vous regardiez, ils regardaient.»² Careful: the endings «ais», «ais», «ait», and «aient» sound the same! Only «ions» and «iez» sound different. Same recipe for every verb. «Nous finissons» gives «je finissais.»² «Nous avons» gives «j'avais.»² «Nous faisons» gives «je faisais.»² And «nous vivons» gives «ils vivaient.»² Remember the spelling: with «nous», the ending «ions» is a «nous» ending, so «nous regardions» keeps the «ons» sound inside it.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: I was watching. -> Je regardais.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: The dinosaurs lived in forests. -> Les dinosaures vivaient dans des forêts.

## Pause ! · Un seul chaos  (malcolm)

The stem is «ét»: «j'étais, tu étais, il était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils étaient.»² «J'étais petit.»² I was small. «Les brachiosaures étaient grands.»² Only the stem is chaotic. The endings are always the same. That is the most boring chaos in history.

- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: It was hot. -> Il faisait chaud.

## Pause ! · Quand l'utiliser ?  (alan)
- **alan (EN)**: Alan again. When do we use the imparfait? Three uses. One: description. How things were. «Il faisait chaud.»² «Il y avait des forêts immenses.»² It is a photo of the past. Two: habits. What you used to do, again and again. «Tous les jours, je creusais.»² Every day I dug, or I used to dig. «Nous avions un chien.»² We had a dog. Three: the stage, the background, an action in progress. «Je regardais la télé...»² I was watching TV. Keep that third one in mind: it will be very useful in a minute.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: Every day, Alan dug. -> Tous les jours, Alan creusait.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: We had a dog. -> Nous avions un chien.

## Pause ! · Négatif et questions  (ellie)
- **ellie (EN)**: Pause! Two small things, and you already know both. The negative and the question work exactly as in the present. The negative wraps around the verb: «ne», the imparfait, «pas». «Le T. rex ne vivait pas au Jurassique.»² The T. rex did not live in the Jurassic. «Je ne regardais pas.»² «Nous n'avions pas d'eau.»² Before a vowel, «ne» becomes «n'», and after a negative, «de l'eau» becomes «pas d'eau». And «il y avait» becomes «il n'y avait pas»: «Il n'y avait pas de montagnes.»² There were no mountains. Questions: «Est-ce que tu aimais ça?»² Did you like that? Or just raise your voice: «Tu habitais à Genk?»² Did you live in Genk? One verb form in the imparfait translates «did», «was» and «used to». Nice and simple.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: T. rex did not live in the Jurassic. -> Le T. rex ne vivait pas au Jurassique.

## Pause ! · Il y a + durée  (ellie)
- **ellie (EN)**: Pause! You know «il y a» means «there is». But «il y a», plus a length of time, means «ago». «Il y a deux jours.»² Two days ago. «Il y a trois ans.»² Three years ago. «Il y a cent ans.»² «Il y a soixante-six millions d'années.»² Sixty-six million years ago. Notice «d'années»: after a big number like «million», you say «de» or «d'». Remember the two jobs of «il y a»: «Il y a un dinosaure»: there is a dinosaur. «Il y a deux jours»: two days ago. And the expression is not changing: it is always «il y a».
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: Sixty-five million years ago. -> Il y a soixante-cinq millions d'années.

## Pause ! · Le décor et l'événement  (alan)
- **alan (EN)**: Pause! Now the big idea of the episode. Two past tenses, two jobs. The imparfait is the stage. It sets the scene: it is long, it is in progress, you do not see its start or its end. Imagine a long line. The passé composé is the event. It is short. It happens at one moment. Imagine a dot on that line. Put them together: «Je dormais quand le T. rex est arrivé.»² I was sleeping, that is the long line, when the T. rex arrived, that is the dot. The stage, then the event. Another one: «Il pleuvait quand nous sommes sortis.»² It was raining, the stage, when we went out, the event. The little word «quand» often joins them. Ask yourself one question: is it the stage, or is it something that happened? Stage: imparfait. Happened: passé composé.
- **alan (EN)**: A tip: signal words. Some small words tell you the tense before you even read the verb. Words for repetition: «tous les jours, toujours, souvent, d'habitude.»² «Toujours» is always, «souvent» is often, «d'habitude» is usually. These call for the imparfait, because a habit repeats. Words for one moment: «un jour, soudain, hier, tout à coup.»² «Soudain» is suddenly. These call for the passé composé, because an event happens once. Compare: «Un jour, le T. rex est arrivé.»² One day, the T. rex arrived. And: «Tous les jours, le T. rex arrivait.»² Every day, the T. rex arrived. Same verb, different meaning, and the tense is how you hear it.
- **alan (EN)**: Let us practise the choice, with four examples. «Alan creusait quand il a trouvé un fossile.»² Alan was digging, the long line, when he found a fossil, the dot. «Ashok regardait le film quand l'électricité est partie.»² The film is the stage, the power cut is the event. «Il faisait chaud et il y avait du soleil. Soudain, un dinosaure est arrivé.»² Two descriptions, then one event. And a habit, then a single moment: «Quand j'étais petit, j'avais un chien. Un jour, il est parti.»² Look: «avais» is a habit and a description, «est parti» is the event. Notice that «parti» is the verb «partir», with «être», as in episode ten.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: Alan was digging when he found a fossil. -> Alan creusait quand il a trouvé un fossile.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: I was sleeping when the T. rex arrived. -> Je dormais quand le T. rex est arrivé.
- **À toi** [ashok]: Say: The raptors had feathers. -> Les raptors avaient des plumes.
