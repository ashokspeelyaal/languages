## Pause ! · Les verbes en -er  (ellie)

- **ellie** (explains, board er1): Pause! The most important board of the day. Hammond said: «Nous visitons le parc.»² «Nous regardons les dinosaures.»² These are verbs that end in «er» in the dictionary: «visiter», «regarder», «parler», «manger». More than ninety percent of French verbs end in «er», and they all follow the same pattern. Learn it once, and you know thousands of verbs. We take «regarder», to look at, to watch. [[+]]Step one: remove the «er». What is left is the radical: «regard». Step two: add an ending. The endings are: e, es, e, ons, ez, ent. Say it with me: «e, es, e, ons, ez, ent.»² [[+]]«Je regarde.»² I look. «Tu regardes.»² «Il regarde.»² «Elle regarde.»² [[+]]«Nous regardons.»² [[+]]«Vous regardez.»² [[+]]«Ils regardent.»² «Elles regardent.»² [[+]]Now listen to the sound. The endings e, es and ent are silent! «Je regarde, tu regardes, il regarde, ils regardent»: four forms, and they all sound the same. Only «nous regardons» and «vous regardez» sound different. So in speech, this is easier than it looks. Another one: «manger», to eat. «Je mange.»² «Tu manges.»² «Il mange.»² «Ils mangent.»² Another: «parler», to speak. «Je parle.»² «Nous parlons.»² «Vous parlez.»² Beautiful.
- À TOI: Say: We are watching the dinosaurs. -> **Nous regardons les dinosaures.**
- À TOI: Say: They are looking for the raptor. -> **Ils cherchent le raptor.**

## Pause ! · Le petit chaos  (malcolm)

- **malcolm** (explains, board er2): Ian Malcolm here. «-er» verbs are the orderly part of French. But even order has two little wrinkles. Look at the board. [[+]]First wrinkle: «nous mangeons.»² The verb «manger» keeps an e before «ons». Why? Because a g before the letter o would sound hard, like in «go». The e keeps the g soft, like the s in «measure». «Nous mangeons.»² «Nous mangeons des plantes.»² The same trick works for «voyager»: «nous voyageons.» [[+]]Second wrinkle: «j'aime.»² Ashok said: «Je aime.» Two vowels bump into each other, and French hates that. So «je» loses its e and becomes «j'», with an apostrophe. «J'aime.»² «J'adore.»² «J'habite.»² The h is silent, so it counts as a vowel. «J'écoute.»² «J'étudie.»² Every verb that starts with a vowel or a silent h does this. [[+]]And one last reminder: «ils mangent» sounds exactly like «il mange». The «ent» is a ghost. You write it; you don't say it.
- À TOI: Say: We eat plants. -> **Nous mangeons des plantes.**

## Pause ! · La négation  (ellie)

- **ellie** (explains, board neg): Pause! Ellie again. Alan said: «Il ne mange pas de viande.»² «Il ne mange pas.» He doesn't eat. This is the French negation, and it is a formula. [[+]]Two little words wrap around the verb, like brackets: «ne», then the verb, then «pas». «Ne, verbe, pas.»² [[+]]Take «je mange». It becomes «je ne mange pas.»² I don't eat. «Ne» goes before the verb, «pas» goes after it. [[+]]«Tu ne regardes pas.»² «Nous ne mangeons pas.»² «Ils ne chassent pas.»² A pronunciation tip: in speech, many French people drop the «ne». But you must write it, and you should say it for now. [[+]]What if the verb starts with a vowel? Same as before: «ne» becomes «n'». «Je n'aime pas.»² I don't like. «Je n'ai pas peur.»² I'm not scared. Yes! Do you remember? «J'ai peur», «je n'ai pas peur.» «Il n'est pas ici.»² He isn't here. «Je ne suis pas fatigué.»² I'm not tired. «Être» and «avoir» work with «ne… pas» too. Let's say a few together: «Je ne suis pas fatigué.»² «Je n'ai pas peur.»² «Je ne mange pas.»² Excellent.

I eat plants: «Je mange des plantes.»² I don't eat plants: «Je ne mange pas de plantes.»² [[+]]I have a jeep: «J'ai une jeep.»² I don't have a jeep: «Je n'ai pas de jeep.»² [[+]]And before a vowel, «de» becomes «d'»: «Il ne mange pas d'animaux.»² Logic: «de» means «of». «Not any of». ## Pause ! · Aimer  (ellie)

- **ellie** (explains, board aimer): A small but important rule, from the verbs of liking. Ashok said: «J'aime les dinosaures.»² «J'adore les dinosaures.»² Three verbs for feelings: «aimer», to like or to love, «adorer», to love, and «détester», to hate. They are all regular «er» verbs. [[+]]«J'aime les dinosaures.»² [[+]]«J'adore la jungle.»² «Je déteste la viande.»² [[+]]And the rule: with these verbs, you use the definite article: «le», «la», «l'», «les». Never «un», «une» or «des». Even when English has no article: «I like dinosaurs» is «j'aime les dinosaures», with «les». [[+]]Compare. To eat: «je mange des plantes.»² To like: «j'aime les plantes.»² With «manger», an amount: «des». With «aimer», the idea in general: «les». Two verbs, two articles. «Tu aimes les plantes?» «Non, je n'aime pas les plantes.»²

## Pause ! · Les questions  (ellie)

- **ellie** (explains, board quest): Pause! In this scene, Ian asked: «Où est le T. rex?»² Let's learn how to ask questions. There are three ways, from easiest to most careful. [[+]]Way number one: intonation. Take a normal sentence and raise your voice at the end. «Tu aimes l'île.» is a statement. «Tu aimes l'île?»² is a question. Nothing else changes. In everyday speech, this is the most common way. «Tu regardes le T. rex?»² «Vous mangez?»² [[+]]Way number two: put «est-ce que» in front of a normal sentence. «Est-ce que tu aimes l'île?»² It is a little marker, almost like «is it true that». «Est-ce que» sounds like «esker». Before a vowel it becomes «est-ce qu'»: «Est-ce qu'il mange?»² «Est-ce qu'elle aime les plantes?»² «Est-ce que vous cherchez le raptor?»² [[+]]Way number three: a question word in front, like «où», where. «Où est le T. rex?»² «Tu habites où?»² A question word can start the sentence, or come at the end. [[+]]And the same meaning: «Tu aimes l'île?», «Est-ce que tu aimes l'île?»² Pick whichever you like.
- **ellie** (explains, board qword): Here are the question words. Say each one with me. [[+]]«Où?»² Where? «Où est le parc?»² [[+]]«Quand?»² When? «Comment?»² How? «Comment tu t'appelles?»² You know that one already! [[+]]«Pourquoi?»² Why? And the answer starts with «parce que»,² because. «Pourquoi tu regardes?» «Parce que j'aime les dinosaures.»² [[+]]«Combien?»² How much, or how many? «Combien de dinosaures?»² «Qui?»² Who? [[+]]And the big one, «qu'est-ce que», which means: what. «Qu'est-ce que tu regardes?»² What are you looking at? «Qu'est-ce que tu manges?»² «Qu'est-ce que tu aimes?»² «Qu'est-ce que», plus a normal sentence. It sounds like «kess ke». Learn it as one block. Now you can ask about almost anything: «Où est le T. rex?»² «Pourquoi tu as peur?»²
- À TOI: Say: Where is the T. rex? -> **Où est le T. rex ?**
- À TOI: Say: Do you like the island? -> **Est-ce que tu aimes l'île ?**
- À TOI: Say: What are you looking at? -> **Qu'est-ce que tu regardes ?**
- À TOI: Say: Why? Because I like dinosaurs. -> **Pourquoi ? Parce que j'aime les dinosaures.**
