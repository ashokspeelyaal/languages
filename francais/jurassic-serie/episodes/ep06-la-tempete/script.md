## Pause ! · L'impératif

- **ellie (EN, board imper)**: Pause! Alan gave orders: «Ne bouge pas!»² «Reste derrière moi!»² «Écoute!»² This is the imperative. The formula is simple: take the «tu» form of the verb, and remove the pronoun. «Tu regardes» becomes «Regarde!»² The pronoun «tu» disappears. And look closely: «regardes» has an S, but «Regarde!» has none. The rule: with -er verbs, the S of the «tu» form is dropped. «Tu restes», «Reste!»² «Tu écoutes», «Écoute!»² For «nous», it means «let's»: «Regardons!»² Let's look. For «vous», you give a polite order, or you speak to several people: «Regardez!»² «Écoutez!»² «Restez ici!»² And the negative order. Wrap the verb in brackets: «ne», plus the imperative, plus «pas». «Ne bouge pas!»² «Ne bougez pas!»² «N'écoute pas!»² 
- **À toi**: Say: Don't move! -> Ne bouge pas !
- **À toi**: Say: Look! To several people. -> Regardez !

## Pause ! · Le chaos des ordres

- **malcolm (EN, board chaos)**: Ian Malcolm here. Of course there are exceptions, Ashok. Chaos never sleeps. First: only the -er verbs lose their S. «Viens!»² «Cours!»² Come! Run! These verbs keep their S, because they are not -er verbs. And «aller» looks like an -er verb, but it is irregular, so it is chaos too. «Tu vas» becomes «Va!»² Go! Without the S. And «Allons-y!»² Let's go! Learn it as one word. And «être», of course, is wild: «Sois calme!»² Be calm. «Soyez calmes!»² Nothing like «tu es». Now repeat, with energy: «Viens! Cours! Allons-y!»²
- **À toi**: Say: Come here! -> Viens ici !
- **À toi**: Say: Let's go! -> Allons-y !

## Pause ! · Il faut

- **ellie (EN, board ilfaut)**: Pause! Malcolm said: «Il faut attendre.»² We have to wait. «Il faut» means: it is necessary, you must, we must. It is followed by an infinitive. «Il faut courir.»² We have to run. «Il faut attendre.»² «Il faut rester.»² And the negative: «Il ne faut pas bouger.»² You must not move. «Il ne faut pas courir.»² It is a block: «il faut» never changes. No «je», no «tu». Very practical.
- **À toi**: Say: We have to run. -> Il faut courir.

## Pause ! · J'ai froid

- **malcolm (EN, board avoir)**: Malcolm again, with Ashok's mistake. He said: «Je suis froid.» In English you say: I AM cold. A French person hears: I am a cold object, like an ice cube. Not good. In French, feelings use «avoir», to have. You do not «are» cold, you «have» cold. «J'ai froid.»² «J'ai chaud.»² «J'ai faim.»² I have hunger, so I am hungry. «J'ai soif.»² I am thirsty. And you already know: «J'ai peur.»² I am afraid. And «Tu as peur?»² Say it: «J'ai froid, j'ai faim, j'ai soif, j'ai peur.»² So «Il fait froid» is the weather, and «J'ai froid» is you.
- **À toi**: Say: I am cold. -> J'ai froid.
- **À toi**: Say: I am hungry. -> J'ai faim.
