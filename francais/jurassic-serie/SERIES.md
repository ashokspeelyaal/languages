# Série « Français Jurassique », A1 → A2 (12 episodes)

These are private learning videos, a fan-made story with Jurassic Park characters. They are watched only by the user (iCloud), never published.

**Rules**
- All dialogue is our own: no film scripts, no famous film quotes, no film stills, logo or music.
- The characters are drawn as our own flat cartoon versions, not actor look-alikes.
- Photos are real and come from Wikimedia Commons: dinosaur skeletons and fossils, tropical islands and jungle (Kauai, Costa Rica), labs, museums.

## The learner
- Ashok, from Tamil Nadu, lives in Belgium, an IT person and a structured learner.
- French: **absolute beginner (A1)**. Goal: A2 by the end of the series.
- Knows the six films well (in English).
- Watches in the gym on an iPhone, so the audio must work on its own, and the on-screen text must be big.

## The conceit
"You" (the viewer, addressed as **tu**, called Ashok) are the newest visitor or intern on Isla Nublar. The characters teach you French while things happen in the park. They speak slow, clear French to you. The grammar explanations are in **English**.

## Characters and grammar roles
Each character always teaches the same kind of thing, so the brain links the grammar to the person.

| Character | Personality in our story | Teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Ellie Sattler** | warm, clear, practical palaeobotanist | **the rules**: formulas on the blackboard, patterns, agreement |
| **Alan Grant** | gruff, loves fossils, dislikes computers, softens over time | **the past**: things that are dug up (passé composé and imparfait in A2); describing dinosaurs |
| **Ian Malcolm** | ironic, funny, chaos theory | **the exceptions and irregular verbs** ("grammar is chaos, but chaos has patterns") |
| **John Hammond** | enthusiastic host, "no expense spared" type (never quote it) | **welcome, numbers, prices, il y a** |
| **Owen Grady** (A2) | calm raptor trainer | **commands, the imperative, pronouns** (le, la, lui) |
| **Claire Dearing** (A2) | organised park manager, schedules | **time, daily routine, reflexive verbs, formal vous** |

## Priorities (from the user)
1. **Grammar teaching is the most important part.** The « Pause ! » boards take about 50–60 % of the time. Build the formulas step by step, give many examples, and repeat.
2. **Slow pace.** Fast speech is hard to memorise. 25 minutes per episode is fine.
3. **Subtitles:** French and English both on screen, in different colours, without disturbing the picture.

## Episode format (20–25 min each, same rhythm every time)
1. **Rappel** (1–2 min): a quick recap of the previous episode with 3–4 "À toi !" questions. Skipped in episode 1.
2. **Scenes** (3–4 per episode): a short scene in a place (photo) at **slow** speed (rate −25 % in A1, −15 % in A2, a 0.9 s pause after every sentence).
   - **Two subtitle lines in two colours.** French on top: bigger, white/yellow, with the karaoke highlight. English underneath: smaller, light cyan.
   - The subtitle bar stays at the bottom. Vocab cards stay at the top right, so they never cover the subtitles or faces.
3. **« Pause ! »** after each scene: the picture freezes and a **blackboard** appears. The rule is shown as a formula (`ne + VERB + pas`, `le + ami → l'ami`), explained in English with 3–5 examples spoken in French.
4. **« À toi ! »:** a prompt in English ("Say: I am tired"), then **4 seconds of silence** with a countdown, then the answer spoken twice.
5. **The scene again:** now the viewer understands everything.
6. **Résumé** (1 min): the episode's formulas on one board, plus 10 key words.
7. **Vocabulary cards** pop up as in the Dutch videos (word plus English, with **le/la** and gender colour: blue = masculine, red = feminine).

## Continuity rules
- Each episode reuses earlier grammar in its dialogues (spiral repetition).
- Characters address the learner as "Ashok" and use **tu** (except Claire, who first uses vous, which is the point of episode 7).
- The French in episode N only uses grammar from episodes 1..N, plus explicitly flagged "chunks" (e.g. "j'ai peur" in episode 2 as a fixed expression).
- No gore or deaths: light tension and fun.
